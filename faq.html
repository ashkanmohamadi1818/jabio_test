Frequently asked questions

Short, honest answers about what Jabio can and cannot do.

What does Jabio do?

You pick a business and a city. Jabio counts the people, customers, access and rivals around every 250 m block and ranks the places; or you click a spot and it tells you what fits there. One documented formula, a report you can print, and the numbers behind every score.

Where does the data come from, and how recent is it?

OpenStreetMap (snapshot of 2026-11-04), Overture Maps, WorldPop and a few others, all open. The full list with licences is on the sources page.

How accurate is it?

It is a shortlist tool, not a prediction of profit. Map data is incomplete (in Tehran roughly half of the real shops are mapped) and residents are a model. Each result shows a plausible range and says if the data around it is thin. Always go and look at the spot. Data sources and licences

Why does a luxury café point to the north of Tehran?

Some kinds of business depend on income. For a luxury café, residents and customers count in proportion to the buying power around, and the income level is part of the score. Income is estimated from ground height and exists for Tehran only, so this effect does not exist in other cities.

The map near me looks empty or thin. Can I help?

Yes. The “Add missing shops” page marks thin areas, lets you walk and record the shops that are missing and download a file, and the “Add a missing place” button opens OpenStreetMap so the whole world gets the shop, not just this site.

Does it include rent or prices?

No. Listing sites forbid automatic collection and we do not copy them. The break-even box lets you type your own rent and see how many customers a day you need.

Which cities does it cover?

Fifteen cities, from Tehran to Urmia. Coverage is marked rich, partial or thin per city. The districts view, the income level and the foot-traffic layer exist only for Tehran (foot traffic for district 5).

What is the foot-traffic layer?

A model of where people walk, built from the street network, homes, shops and stops. It is relative (100 = the busiest 2% of streets in district 5), and nobody has counted pedestrians yet to calibrate it. Count people at your own spot before relying on it.

Is my information stored?

Analysis runs in your browser. Guest saves stay on your device; signed-in saves are stored in your account. Details on the privacy page. Privacy

I found a mistake, or I want something added. Who do I tell?

Use the contact page and say which city, business and spot you tried. A wrong shop on the map is best fixed in OpenStreetMap itself. Contact
