What this data cannot tell you

  • Places are incomplete. In Tehran OpenStreetMap holds roughly half of the real shops, and Overture can still list a shop that has closed. Every result says whether the map data around it is rich, partial or thin.
  • Residents are a model. In Tehran the pattern is WorldPop blended half-and-half with building footprints; against the district populations we could find published, the typical error is about 1.7 times. District residents can be off by a factor of two or more.
  • Income level exists for Tehran only, and it is a proxy from ground height, smoothed over about 750 m. It cannot see a rich pocket in a poor area.
  • There is no rent, no price and no real foot-traffic count. The foot-traffic layer is a model for district 5 of Tehran only, relative, and no pedestrian has been counted to calibrate it.
  • The weights of the score are judgement settings, not fitted to outcomes. The check we ran only shows the score agrees with where businesses already are, not that a place will be profitable.
  • A score is a shortlist to go and look at, not a verdict. Visit the spot, count the people and ask the neighbours before you sign a lease.
