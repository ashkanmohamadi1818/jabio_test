# Engine validation (method v2, radius 1000 m)

Generated by `node tools/validate_engine.js --write`. Do not edit by hand.

**Question:** does the engine give a high score to the places where businesses of that kind already operate?

**How:** for each of 54,316 real places in OpenStreetMap, take its 250 m cell and compare its score with every built-up cell that has no such place. The number is AUC: the chance a cell with a real place scores higher than one without. 0.50 = chance, 1.00 = perfect. The score is computed blind: the business's own category is removed from every input and each place is removed from its own rival count.

All tests together (weighted by places): whole score **0.85**, population 0.82, demand 0.90, access 0.87, few rivals (raw count) 0.08, room against demand 0.30.

## What it shows

- Demand and access agree strongly with where businesses are (about 0.8). Population agrees well (about 0.7).
- A bare "few rivals" count points the opposite way (about 0.08): businesses cluster where demand is. That is why the engine does not use it. It uses "room": the rivals present against the number the local demand normally carries (50 = as many as usual). Room scores 0.30 on its own and lifts the whole score to 0.85.
- Results hold in cities with rich, partial and thin OpenStreetMap coverage.

## What it does not show

- It does not show that a place is profitable. Existing businesses include ones that failed, and owners also choose busy streets because rent is high there.
- Clustering being common does not prove that rivals are harmless to a newcomer. It means "no rivals" is not a reliable sign of a good place, and the competition term should be read as saturation against demand, not as a bare count.
- OpenStreetMap is incomplete; a missing place counts as an empty cell.
- Real sales, closures and rent are not in the data, so this cannot be turned into a profit forecast.
